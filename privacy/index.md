# Ribbon Journal Privacy Policy

_Effective October 3, 2026._

**In short:** Ribbon Journal doesn't collect any data. What you write stays on your
devices and, if you use iCloud, in your own iCloud account. The developer
can't see it.

## What Ribbon Journal stores, and where

**On your device.** Your pages (handwriting, typed text and dates), your
notebooks and their settings, the text Ribbon Journal reads from your handwriting so
you can search it, and your preferences. Your pages, notebooks and search
text are kept in files with iOS Data Protection: they can't be read while
your device is locked.

**In your iCloud account, if you use it.** When you're signed in to iCloud
and iCloud is on for Ribbon Journal, your pages and notebooks are copied to your
private iCloud database so your other devices can show them. Apple stores
this data under its own privacy policy. The developer has no access to it.
The text read from your handwriting isn't uploaded: each device reads your
pages itself.

**Nowhere else.** Ribbon Journal has no accounts, no servers of its own, no
analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code. It doesn't
log what you write.

## Handwriting recognition

Ribbon Journal reads your handwriting on your device, with Apple's built-in
recognizers, to make your pages searchable and to describe them to VoiceOver.
Your writing isn't sent to the developer or anyone else to do this.

## Face ID and passcode

If you lock your journal, iOS checks your Face ID or passcode. Ribbon Journal only
learns whether the check succeeded; it never sees your face data or passcode.
The lock covers the journal on screen; your files are protected by Data
Protection whether the lock is on or not.

## Exporting

When you export pages as a PDF, the file is made on your device and goes only
where you choose to send it. Ribbon Journal deletes its temporary copy afterwards.

## Erasing and deleting

So that syncing between devices can never lose your writing, Ribbon Journal hides
things rather than destroying them:

- Erased ink stays in the page's file, hidden.
- A deleted notebook waits in Recently Deleted with its pages, and can be
  restored.
- Each page keeps up to 20 earlier versions on your device, so a damaged
  file can be recovered.

To remove everything for good, delete the app (this removes everything it
stored on your device), then delete Ribbon Journal's data from iCloud: in Settings,
open your Apple Account's iCloud storage, choose Ribbon Journal and delete its data.

## Crash reports

If you've chosen in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers,
Apple may send the developer anonymous crash reports. These describe where
the app failed, not what you wrote.

## Children

Ribbon Journal isn't directed at children, and it doesn't collect data from anyone.
